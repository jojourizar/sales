# Playbook: ENTERPRISE motion (large accounts, public sector, committee sales)

Large accounts or configured strategic segments. Committee purchase, long cycle, large renewal. The expensive mistake here is underestimating the number of decision makers.

## Buying committee you should have mapped
- Economic decision maker (signs the budget: CFO, finance director, budget authority).
- Technical champion or main user (the person who lives the problem daily).
- Procurement or purchasing (formal buyer, especially in public sector).
- Executive sponsor (leadership or board).
- Potential blockers (legal, IT, an installed competitor).

## Cadence benchmark
- First touch after discovery: within 24 to 48 hours.
- Multi-threading is mandatory: at least 3 people in the committee with their own interaction.
- 8 to 12 touches per quarter across meetings, email and tailored material.
- A meeting every 2 to 3 weeks up to proposal.

## Materials to prepare
- A quantified ROI one-pager for the economic buyer.
- Business case and impact analysis for leadership.
- Technical demo and a pilot for the champion.
- Implementation plan and written service level agreement.
- Response to any formal RFP or procurement requirements.

## Coverage checklist (all must be yes before proposing)
- [ ] Economic decision maker identified and reachable.
- [ ] Decision process documented (who approves, in what order, on what calendar).
- [ ] Internal champion confirmed who sells for you inside the account.
- [ ] At least 3 committee stakeholders with recorded interaction.
- [ ] Decision criteria and installed competition known.

## Resource allocation
Top priority weighted by lifetime value. Justify involving leadership, technical pre-sales, and legal. Future renewal and expansion offset the cost to serve. Never delegate multi-threading to a single person.
